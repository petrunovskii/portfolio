Yandex, January to February 2025

Open-source launch of Perforator for developers and business media

The repository became the top new repository on GitHub on its launch day and gained 2,038 stars within five days. The launch received coverage in Russia, Kazakhstan, India and China.

View case study

Context

The company released Perforator, the continuous profiling system behind its search and advertising services, as open-source software. The tool identifies the code that consumes the most server resources. The subject has limited appeal outside systems engineering.

Task

The launch had to reach developers, who would evaluate the code, and business media, which required a commercial reason to cover the release.

My role

I led public relations for the launch and prepared the master document, the messaging framework, the press release, the Q&A and the pool of external experts.

What I did

  • Developed two lines of messaging. Business media received the cost argument of up to 20% lower server expenses, and developers received the engineering detail, including eBPF, low overhead and support for C++, Go, Rust, Python and Java.
  • Assembled a pool of external experts who provided independent commentary to journalists.
  • Coordinated distribution outside Russia with the international communications team.
  • Gathered the facts for and edited the article on the company blog on Habr.

Results

  • The repository ranked first among new repositories on GitHub on 30 January 2025, according to Changelog Nightly.
  • The repository gained 2,038 stars by 3 February.
  • Coverage appeared in Habr, CNews and Sostav in Russia, Kapital.kz and Digital Business in Kazakhstan, CXOToday in India and ifanr in China.
  • The Habr article reached 67,000 readers and collected 56 comments.

Conclusion

A quantified business benefit secured the interest of business media, while the technical article on Habr and the repository itself engaged developers.

Habr · CNews · Sostav · Changelog Nightly · Habr article

Avito Tech, July 2025 to May 2026

Customer case studies for Trisigma, a B2B SaaS platform in its first year on the market

CNews published three articles within four months. Two enterprise customers spoke on the record in case studies based on my interviews with their chief product officers.

View case study

Context

Trisigma is an A/B testing platform that the company developed for internal use and has offered to other companies since March 2025. Buyers of such platforms rely on the experience of peer companies more than on vendor claims.

Task

The first external customers had recently adopted the product and could not yet report annual results, while the product required market evidence immediately.

My role

I was responsible for public relations of the product, including the annual plan, customer case studies, press releases and the tracker of awards and events.

What I did

  • Designed a two-part case study format. The first part describes the reasons for adopting experimentation and the implementation process, and the second part, published a year later, reports the results.
  • Conducted interviews with the chief product officers of Burger King Russia and Detsky Mir, a national children's goods retailer, and wrote both articles.
  • Prepared and placed the press release on the Virtual Private Cloud delivery model for customers with strict security requirements.
  • Maintained a pipeline of further customers for the series.
  • Organised a video interview with the Trisigma CEO for the AviTalk show, prepared the talking points and co-directed filming.

Results

  • CNews published the Burger King Russia case study on 23 December 2025.
  • CNews published the Detsky Mir case study on 27 February 2026.
  • CNews and Telecom Daily covered the Virtual Private Cloud delivery model on 17 April 2026.
  • The AviTalk interview with the Trisigma CEO has 2,739 views on YouTube.

Conclusion

Customer interviews also provided information on the barriers to adoption within client organisations, which is valuable to the product team as well as to communications.

Avito, August to October 2025

Integrated campaign for avito.tech.conf, the company's first engineering management conference

The conference attracted about 1,000 on-site guests and 10,000 online viewers. The campaign generated 1,627 registrations at about RUB 1,100 each within a budget of RUB 8 to 9 million, or approximately $100,000.

View case study

Context

The company held its first conference for engineering managers on 17 October 2025 in Moscow. The target audience is narrow and consists of team leads and heads of engineering, most of whom work for other large technology companies.

Task

A new event has neither an established audience nor a media history. The campaign therefore had to reach the audience near their offices, in coffee shops and in the Telegram channels they read.

My role

I managed the paid campaign on the client side. Two agencies handled media buying and negotiations, while I set their tasks and made decisions on channels, locations and copy. The coffee shops were selected jointly with the team, and media relations were shared with two colleagues.

What I did

  • Placed seven billboards next to the offices of major technology employers in Moscow.
  • Developed a limited-edition drink, Lead's flip, with the specialty roaster The Welder Catherine. The drink was sold in four coffee shops, and about a dozen partner coffee shops used branded cups.
  • Arranged 40 placements in Telegram channels for IT managers to drive registrations, together with personal invitations published by eight opinion leaders.
  • Ran a teaser wave in major technology and city channels about unusual billboards that had appeared opposite the offices of technology companies, followed by a second wave that announced the programme. The two waves comprised 22 placements.
  • Prepared the announcement press release, invited about 60 journalists, managed accreditation and ran the press room on the day of the event.
  • Organised the video recording of a conference talk on launching LLM projects, prepared the talking points and co-directed filming.

Results

  • About 1,000 guests attended on site and more than 10,000 people watched online.
  • About 30 journalists attended the event.
  • The registration placements generated 1,627 registrations.
  • The campaign received 1.38 million views in Telegram, of which 1.1 million came from the teaser and the announcement.
  • A second edition was announced for September 2026.

Conclusion

The billboards delivered their greatest effect as a subject for publications, since the posts about them received 700,000 views. In future campaigns the teaser should be planned first and the outdoor placements selected to support it.

Avito, August 2025 to February 2026

Telegram influencer programme across eight campaigns

The programme delivered 104 placements and 4.4 million views at an average cost of RUB 2.2 per view. The total budget was about RUB 10 million, or approximately $120,000.

View case study

Context

In Russia, Telegram channels are the primary route to technology and business audiences. A paid placement operates in the same way as influencer marketing, since the channel author presents the news in an individual voice.

Task

Each launch required a different audience, such as developers for Developer Day, city residents for a fintech launch and IT managers for the conference. A single fixed list of channels would have been inefficient.

My role

I managed eight campaigns from brief to report, including key messages, channel selection, approval of each post and analysis of results. The agency conducted negotiations with channel owners.

What I did

  • Selected channels according to the subject of each campaign, with news and city channels for the instalment launch and technology and management channels for the conference.
  • Maintained a single report for all placements with views, cost per view and cost per thousand subscribers.
  • Promoted the company's documentary about its engineering platform in February 2026 with a second agency, through 11 placements in channels for senior engineers and team leads and video advertising on VK.

Results

  • The eight campaigns delivered 104 placements and 4.4 million views in channels with a combined audience of 44.5 million subscribers.
  • The average cost per view was RUB 2.2. The instalment launch was the most efficient campaign, with 763,000 views at RUB 1.3 per view.
  • Telegram views for the documentary exceeded the forecast by 45%, and the film received 126,000 views on VK Video.

Conclusion

Reports based on subscriber numbers overstate the effect of a placement. Cost per view is a more reliable basis for planning.

Yandex, February to March 2025

Developer communications for the launch of YandexGPT 5

The launch was covered by 23 outlets, including RBC and Kommersant. The open Lite model received 122 likes on Hugging Face in its first week.

View case study

Context

On 25 February 2025 the company released YandexGPT 5 Pro, integrated it into its voice assistant and cloud platform, and published the weights of the smaller Lite model. This was the company's first open model since 2022.

Task

Developers are the most demanding audience for a model launch. They examine benchmarks, read the licence and ask how the model was trained.

My role

I worked in the launch team and was responsible for the developer community track and for the plan covering the weeks after the launch. Media pitching was carried out by the whole team.

What I did

  • Prepared key messages for technical communities and the brief for seeding in developer channels.
  • Prepared the Q&A for technical questions.
  • Developed a plan of 11 post-launch initiatives, including a Habr article on training optimisation, a podcast episode, presentations for university students, a prompting guide and research cases based on the open model. About 70% of the plan was delivered.
  • Gathered the facts for and edited the launch article on the company blog on Habr.
  • Organised the podcast episode from the post-launch plan, prepared the talking points and co-directed filming.

Results

  • Kommersant covered the open release of the Lite model on 25 February 2025.
  • The launch was covered by 23 outlets, including an RBC exclusive and Kommersant, which is a result of the whole team.
  • Eight further technology outlets covered the open-source release.
  • The Lite model received 122 likes on Hugging Face in the first week and more than 200 subsequently, with 26 community discussions.
  • The podcast episode from the post-launch plan, released on 10 April 2025, has 114,000 views on YouTube.
  • The Habr launch article reached 130,000 readers and collected 144 comments.

Conclusion

Media assess a model on the launch day, whereas developers form their opinion over the following month. A separate plan for developers should be ready before the release.

Avito Tech, July 2025

Announcement of the EXPF Sigma acquisition

Kommersant covered the deal in its print edition on 4 July 2025, followed by Inc. Russia, ComNews and Habr. I delivered the announcement in my fifth week at the company.

View case study

The company acquired Sigma, an experimentation platform developed by the analytics company EXPF, to complement its own platform Trisigma.

Task

The announcement had to explain why one company needs two A/B testing products and to reassure existing Sigma customers.

  • Explained the two products by their target users. Sigma serves teams that are beginning to run experiments, and Trisigma serves companies that run hundreds of tests in parallel.

Results

  • Kommersant published the story in its print edition on 4 July 2025.
  • Inc. Russia, ComNews, Habr, iGuides and Oborot.ru also covered the announcement.

Conclusion

In an acquisition announcement, the consequences for existing customers should be addressed first.

Yandex, January 2025

Reactive messaging on open models after the DeepSeek release

After the release of the DeepSeek models in January 2025 I worked with the team on the company's position on open language models for questions from media and the developer community.

View case study

Context

In January 2025 the Chinese company DeepSeek released open models comparable to leading commercial solutions at a considerably lower reported training cost. The release raised public questions about whether large companies still needed their own models and how they should treat open ones.

Task

Give spokespeople a consistent and accurate position before the question reached the company through journalists and the developer community.

My role

I worked on the communications messages together with the PR team, based on the technical analysis from the ML team.

What I did

  • Translated the technical assessment of the DeepSeek models into public statements.
  • Built the position around three messages. Open models strengthen competition and benefit the industry. The company's expertise in pre-training and fine-tuning allows it to use open solutions effectively. The company contributes to open source itself.
  • Recorded the statements the company would not make, so that spokespeople avoided commitments not agreed with the product teams.

Results

  • The company's position on open models was ready in January 2025, about a week after the DeepSeek R1 release.

Conclusion

In reactive communications an accurate position matters more than speed. A clear list of what the company does not say protects spokespeople as much as the key messages do.
